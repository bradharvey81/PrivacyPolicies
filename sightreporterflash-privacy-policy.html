The short version

Your photos, your notes and your coordinates stay on your phone. There is no SightReporter Flash account, and no SightReporter Flash server for them to go to.

Two things do leave the device. Your approximate coordinates go to a map provider to fetch the satellite inset on the stamp, and — if you use the dictation buttons — your voice goes to your phone's own speech service. Nothing else does, unless you share it yourself.

No account No analytics No advertising Nothing sold

What the app handles

SightReporter Flash takes a photograph, burns a stamp into it — the time, your coordinates, a description you type or dictate, your business logo and a satellite inset — and saves both the original and the stamped copy to your phone. Everything in the table below exists to do that job.

None of it is uploaded to us. There is no sign-up, no login and no cloud sync in the app.

What leaves your device

Exactly three services receive anything, and one more receives whatever you choose to send it. Your photographs and your notes are not part of any of the first three.

4.1 Satellite imagery (Esri)

To draw the satellite inset on the stamp, the app requests map tiles from Esri's ArcGIS World Imagery service at server.arcgisonline.com over an encrypted HTTPS connection. That request necessarily reveals the map tile you are looking at, which corresponds to your approximate position, along with your IP address and the ordinary technical details any web request carries.

The request contains a location and nothing else — no photograph, no note, no logo, no device identifier and no account. Imagery is fetched only when the app has a location fix and a network connection, and each tile is cached on your phone so the same place is not requested twice. If you deny location permission, no request is ever made. Esri's handling of these requests is governed by Esri's privacy statement.

4.2 Purchases (Google Play Billing)

The Pro upgrade is sold through Google Play. When you buy it, the entire transaction is handled by the Google Play Store app: we never see your name, payment card, billing address or Google account. The app receives only a yes-or-no answer about whether this install holds the upgrade, and caches that answer locally so it survives being offline.

4.3 Voice dictation (your device's speech service)

Worth knowing

The dictation buttons use Android's built-in speech recognition. On most phones that service is provided by Google, and unless your device is set up for on-device recognition, your speech is sent to that provider's servers to be transcribed. This happens under the provider's privacy policy, not ours — we neither receive nor store the audio. If you would rather nothing left the device, type the description instead; every feature works the same either way.

Which service handles it depends on your phone and your settings. You can check under Settings › System › Languages & input › On-device speech recognition on most devices.

4.4 Anything you share yourself

When you tap Share in the gallery, Android hands the stamped photographs to whichever app you pick — email, a messaging app, cloud storage, your client's portal. From that moment the receiving app's privacy policy applies, and the stamp's coordinates, timestamp and description travel with the image because they are burnt into the pixels. Consider that before sharing a photo of a private address.

Separately, if you turn on the camera-roll option in Settings, copies land in DCIM/Camera — the folder Google Photos and similar apps back up by default. That backup is your own arrangement with that provider, but it is worth knowing the option changes where your photos end up.

Keeping and deleting data

Because your data stays on your phone, retention is something you control directly rather than something you have to ask us to do.

  • Photographs stay until you delete them, in the app's gallery or in your phone's photo app. Deleting a photograph deletes its note and its coordinates with it.
  • Capture timestamps used to meter the Free tier expire by themselves 24 hours after the capture. Deliberately, deleting a photo does not return a capture to your daily allowance — otherwise the limit could be undone with a long-press.
  • Cached satellite tiles live in the app's cache and can be cleared at any time from Settings › Apps › SightReporter Flash › Storage › Clear cache. Android also clears them on its own when the phone needs space.
  • Uninstalling the app removes its private storage — every note, coordinate, logo, timestamp and cached tile. Photographs already saved to your camera roll or to the SightReporter Flash album stay in your photo library; delete them there if you want them gone.

We hold no copy of any of it, so there is nothing on our side to retain or delete. If you email us with a question, we keep that correspondence only as long as it takes to answer you.

Security

Notes, coordinates, your logo and the Pro flag live in the app's private storage, which Android's sandbox keeps out of reach of other apps, and which is covered by your phone's own device encryption. Satellite imagery is requested over HTTPS.

Two honest limits. Photographs are saved to your phone's shared photo library so your gallery and other tools can see them — which means any app you have granted photo access can also see them. And a rooted device, or one someone else can unlock, exposes app storage to anyone with that access. Keeping a screen lock on the phone is the single most useful thing you can do here.

No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

Children's privacy

SightReporter Flash is a general-audience tool built for site photography and field documentation. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 13 — nor, in fact, do we knowingly collect personal information from anyone, since the app sends none to us.

In the EEA and the UK, where processing rests on consent, that consent must be given by a parent or guardian for children below the applicable age of digital consent (between 13 and 16 depending on the country). Parents and guardians should note that the app's camera, microphone and location features are the same ones any camera app exposes, and are governed by the device's own permission controls.

If you believe a child has used the app in a way that concerns you, the remedy is entirely on the device: deleting the photographs and uninstalling the app removes everything. You are welcome to contact us at brad81@gmail.com with any question about this.

Your rights

The unusual thing about a local-only app is that you exercise most privacy rights yourself, immediately, without going through us: the data is on your phone, and you can view, correct, export or destroy it whenever you like. The sections below set out the formal position under the laws that apply.

9.1 Australia

We handle personal information in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles. In practice: we collect only what the app needs to stamp a photograph (APP 3); we use it for no purpose other than the one it was collected for (APP 6); we do not disclose it to overseas recipients except the map request described in section 4.1 (APP 8); we take reasonable steps to protect it as described in section 7 (APP 11); and this policy is our statement of open and transparent management (APP 1).

You have the right to seek access to, and correction of, personal information we hold about you (APPs 12 and 13) — in the ordinary case we hold none, because it never leaves your device. If you are unhappy with how we have handled your privacy, write to us first at brad81@gmail.com; we will respond within 30 days. If our response does not resolve it, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner at oaic.gov.au.

9.2 European Economic Area and United Kingdom

Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, our lawful bases are these. Camera, storage and note-taking are processed to perform the contract you enter into by installing and using the app (Art. 6(1)(b)). Location and microphone are processed on your consent, given through the Android permission prompt and withdrawable at any time in system settings (Art. 6(1)(a)). Confirming a Pro purchase with Google Play rests on contract and on our legitimate interest in ensuring the upgrade you paid for is honoured (Art. 6(1)(f)).

You have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, and the right to withdraw consent at any time without affecting processing already carried out. Because we hold no copy of your data, you satisfy every one of these directly on the device: the gallery shows you everything (access), notes are editable (rectification), deletion and uninstall are immediate (erasure), the stamped JPEG is a standard portable file (portability), and revoking a permission stops the processing (objection and withdrawal). Write to us if you would like help doing any of this.

International transfers. We transfer nothing ourselves. The map tile request in section 4.1 reaches Esri, and the speech recognition in section 4.3 may reach a provider such as Google, both of which may process outside the EEA or UK under their own safeguards.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with your local supervisory authority, or with the UK Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

9.3 California

Under the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the CPRA, the categories of personal information the app handles on your device are: geolocation data, audio information (during dictation only) and visual information (your photographs). We collect none of it ourselves, we have not sold or shared personal information in the preceding twelve months, and we do not use or disclose sensitive personal information beyond the purposes permitted under §7027(m) of the CCPA regulations.

You have the right to know, delete, correct and opt out, and the right not to be discriminated against for exercising any of them. Since we hold no personal information, a request to know or delete will be answered by confirming that fact; deletion of the data itself is done on your device as described in section 6. Requests go to brad81@gmail.com and are answered within 45 days.
